Draft · Building the data foundation to evaluate AI copilots

TELUS Digital / Poatek · Draft

DRAFT — Working draft for editorial review.

AI adoption needed a data foundation

Following the rollout of AI copilots with call-center agents required more than making a tool available. The project needed a platform to organize the data and support analysis of how those copilots were used.

At TELUS, I led an eleven-person team building that foundation on GCP. The recorded scope includes ingestion, enrichment, validation, data quality and observability: the stages between source data and analytical consumption.

Sources for this section: Current professional editorial inventory — AI copilots analytics.

From interactions to metrics

An August 2026 backfill record lists workflows for calls, chats, their enriched forms, consolidated interactions, copilot metrics and a wide consumption table. That sequence gives the story an operational foundation: distinct stages had to execute and be tracked.

The same record notes a dependency on the copilot team for an August run. It establishes an operational dependency, not evidence of an incident, data loss or a widespread delay. No conversation records or agent-level data are reproduced here.

Sources for this section: BACKFILLS — August 2026.

The foundation served several ways of working with data

Alongside the platform, the team built two analytics products and two internal tools. One supported data annotation against a taxonomy; the other let users ask questions over the data in natural language.

They addressed different needs. Annotation provided a structured way to classify the data; natural-language access offered another route to exploration. The available account does not specify the model, retrieval method or authorization design, so those details remain for review.

Sources for this section: Current professional editorial inventory — AI copilots analytics.

The outcome we can already describe

The documented outcome is the platform, products and tools built by an eleven-person team. My contribution was leadership of data and AI delivery in a project connecting engineering, quality and analytical use.

We do not yet have validated measurements of productivity gains, handling-time reduction or satisfaction improvements caused by the copilots. The story can explain how we made evaluation possible without claiming those downstream outcomes.

Sources for this section: Current professional editorial inventory — AI copilots analytics, BACKFILLS — August 2026.

Review before publication

Recover the two products’ names and purposes, the actual metrics and the division of responsibilities. An October 2026 CX Reporting discussion concerns a later platform evolution; it has not been used to retrospectively assign Airflow, dbt or architecture choices to this case.
